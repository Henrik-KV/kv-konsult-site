# Mobile Art Direction

Desktop and mobile are one task. A component is incomplete until both compositions have been designed, rendered, and criticised.

## Compose mobile deliberately

For every major section, decide rather than inherit:

- the first visible idea;
- heading width, scale, and line breaks;
- whether evidence appears before or after explanation;
- content order and what can be shortened;
- media crop, scale, aspect ratio, and edge treatment;
- spacing rhythm and intentional pauses;
- navigation and touch interaction;
- scroll choreography and reduced-motion behaviour.

Do not preserve desktop columns as a long sequence of equal blocks. Change hierarchy, order, density, and motion when the smaller canvas requires it.

## Required visual checks

1. Render desktop.
2. Render mobile.
3. Capture both.
4. Compare hierarchy.
5. Inspect awkward Swedish wrapping and long words.
6. Inspect dead space and accidental density.
7. Inspect content order.
8. Inspect touch targets and menu behaviour.
9. Inspect animation and reduced motion.
10. Revise both versions before continuing.

Test at 390×844 during section work. Before release, also test 320×568, 375×812, 430×932, 768×1024, and landscape mobile.

## Reject mobile when

- it is only stacked desktop content;
- the heading consumes the screen without enough meaning;
- proof is pushed too far below generic explanation;
- the scroll story relies on desktop pinning or excessive height;
- navigation feels like a collapsed library default;
- touch targets, forms, or content order are awkward;
- automated overflow checks pass but the composition is visually weak.
