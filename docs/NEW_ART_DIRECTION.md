# New art direction comparison

All current concepts use the owner-approved colour family: white, living light blue/cyan, deep KV blue and dark blue text. Beige, brown, burgundy, orange and warm editorial palettes are rejected and removed from the rendered concepts.

Comparison board: [desktop and mobile overview](art-directions/art-directions-overview.png)

Per-screenshot rejection review: [ART_DIRECTION_SCREENSHOT_REVIEW.md](ART_DIRECTION_SCREENSHOT_REVIEW.md)

No direction is approved yet. Direction D is the current recommendation for owner review, not a licence to implement Gate 2.

## Direction A — Editorial consultancy

### Composition

- Desktop hero: a long natural statement spans the page; a single Lägesbild proof panel enters from the lower right.
- Mobile hero: copy becomes a compact editorial opening, followed immediately by the product panel.
- Service section: three capabilities are rows in one explanation, not cards.
- Proof section: one large screen spread gives Lägesbild the dominant space.
- CTA: a plain, direct explanation of the first conversation.

### Motion concept

Product screens enter with slow crop changes while the type remains stable. No diagram or pinned process. Reduced motion shows the final composition immediately.

### Rejection risks

- Serif typography can feel like a generic premium-editorial treatment.
- The headline is still large and could become another art-directed slogan if the copy is shortened further.
- This direction is calmer than the owner’s request for a living neon-light-blue experience.

### Visual review

- Strongest: calm hierarchy and restrained proof panel.
- Weakest: the large serif statement remains close to a familiar design-studio pattern.
- Generic signals: oversized editorial headline.
- Desktop: coherent but slightly formal.
- Mobile: readable; Lägesbild remains visible early.
- Safe to show client: no, concept only.

## Direction B — Work archive

### Composition

- Desktop hero: a narrow explanation rail sits beside an asymmetric wall of verified screens.
- Mobile hero: explanation and the first product screen form one continuous archive entry.
- Service section: a ledger explains when to enter through Utbilda, Införa or Bygga.
- Proof section: Lägesbild receives a dedicated case surface.
- CTA: text and contact details use simple rules, not rounded buttons.

### Motion concept

The work wall behaves like a contact sheet: one artefact enlarges as its factual caption becomes readable, then returns to the archive. No fake status or console labels.

### Rejection risks

- The image wall can still become a grid of equal modules if proportions are not kept asymmetric.
- KV currently has too little verified work for a large archive to remain varied.
- The narrow sans-serif treatment can feel like a portfolio index rather than a consultancy.

### Visual review

- Strongest: verified work is the first argument.
- Weakest: large dead space remains in the mobile hero before the first screen.
- Generic signals: large sans headline and project-grid conventions.
- Desktop: evidence hierarchy is strongest of the four.
- Mobile: needs a shorter opening and earlier visible screen.
- Safe to show client: no, concept only.

## Direction C — Human technical studio

### Composition

- Desktop hero: the company is presented as a smaller team; names, explanation and a real app share the opening.
- Mobile hero: the team names become the first human proof rather than three numbered contact rows later.
- Service section: a concrete illustrative assignment explains how training, implementation and building may connect.
- Proof section: a single Lägesbild case combines a large screen with plain facts.
- CTA: direct email routes replace a generic form-first ending.

### Motion concept

Names and work material crossfade in one composed field. With authentic photography, images would replace the current typographic team fallback. Motion never invents people or workshop scenes.

### Rejection risks

- Without authentic team photography, the blue name block remains a placeholder-like device.
- The large serif opening overlaps Direction A too much.
- Numbered steps in the service scenario must be used only because the scenario is sequential.

### Visual review

- Strongest: people enter early and direct contact feels credible.
- Weakest: the non-photographic team fallback is not emotionally rich enough.
- Generic signals: large serif statement and process sequence.
- Desktop: warmer in tone without leaving the approved palette.
- Mobile: better human hierarchy, but the first text block is still long.
- Safe to show client: no, authentic photos are missing.

## Direction D — Luminöst arbete

### Composition

- Desktop hero: a white field, luminous cyan light and a faint KV letterform frame real Lägesbild screens.
- Mobile hero: copy, verified status and product screens remain in the first visual sequence without reproducing desktop columns.
- Service section: capabilities form an uneven continuum rather than three cards.
- Proof section: the luminous field recedes while real product screens become fully readable.
- CTA: a solid cyan field provides a clear end without a rounded marketing button.

### Motion concept

A controlled cyan light pass reveals verified screens through the large KV letterform. As the user scrolls, the screen crops move from layered fragments to legible, full product states. The transformation is evidence becoming inspectable, not chaos becoming a generic node diagram.

### Rejection risks

- Light bloom can drift back into generic technology glow.
- The large KV outline can become decoration if it does not control the screen reveal.
- The bold sans headline still needs a less slogan-like final copy treatment.

### Visual review

- Strongest: closest alignment with the requested white/light-blue energy and the original site’s recognisable luminosity.
- Weakest: still adjacent to tech-studio visual language; restraint is essential.
- Generic signals: oversized sans headline and luminous screen layering.
- Desktop: clearest relationship between brand colour and real work.
- Mobile: strongest first-screen energy, but copy needs one shorter line and proof must rise slightly.
- Safe to show client: no, concept only.

## Recommendation

Take Direction D into owner review, with Direction B’s proof-first discipline. Do not merge the two into a busy hybrid. Gate 2 should implement only the navigation and hero after explicit owner approval.

Claude Design was not available in the current toolset. No paid design tool or asset was used.

- Technical verdict: partial. The isolated prototypes render at desktop and mobile; no application implementation or production build was part of this gate.
- Visual verdict: partial. The palette now matches the owner’s white/light-blue requirement, but every direction retains a stated rejection risk.
- Client-readiness verdict: not safe to show as a finished redesign.

The `visual-design-review`, `mobile-art-direction`, and `anti-ai-template-review` checklists were applied. None of the four directions receives automatic approval.
