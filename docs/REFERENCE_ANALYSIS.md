# Reference analysis

References were selected across consultancy, product studio, architecture, public-sector technology, and independent studio categories. They are not a style shopping list. The purpose is to identify transferable principles and visible failure modes.

Local viewport captures are stored in [`docs/reference-board`](reference-board/).

## Bakken & Bæck — work as the opening argument

Source: [Bakken & Bæck work](https://bakkenbaeck.com/work)

- Strong: the work index is immediate, large and specific. Real projects occupy more space than agency claims.
- Avoid copying: the studio has a large archive and recognised clients; KV cannot imitate that volume or implied scale.
- Template avoidance: different projects use different media and proportions rather than one repeated case card.
- Typography: direct sans-serif copy with little decorative language.
- Proof: artefacts and project names appear in the first viewport.
- Mobile: the portfolio becomes a readable visual sequence rather than a desktop grid squeezed smaller.
- Motion: media carries movement; labels remain subordinate.

## Norgram — asymmetry and strong authorship

Source: [Norgram](https://www.norgram.co/)

- Strong: radical asymmetry makes the studio recognisable without relying on decorative cards.
- Avoid copying: extreme whitespace, tiny metadata and experimental navigation would reduce clarity for municipal and management audiences.
- Template avoidance: one project and one composition dominate instead of equal sections.
- Typography: scale contrast and placement do more than colour.
- Proof: selected project material is the primary surface.
- Mobile: must be checked carefully; the desktop concept cannot simply be compressed.
- Motion: project transitions are part of navigation, not an animation layer over a standard page.

## Manyone — expressive type with substantial client proof

Source: [Manyone Sweden](https://manyone.com/se/)

- Strong: expressive serif type and deep brand colour create a recognisable voice; verified work follows quickly.
- Avoid copying: broad consultancy language, full-screen video, and the volume of major-client proof are inappropriate for KV.
- Template avoidance: editorial type and project media share the hero rather than a diagram of the service.
- Typography: a serif voice contrasts with practical navigation.
- Proof: large client stories support the broad positioning.
- Mobile: the observed hero video produced an almost blank first screen while loading; KV must not make the offer dependent on video.
- Motion: video has narrative weight, but a strong still frame is mandatory.

## Snøhetta — projects first, minimal interface

Source: [Snøhetta](https://www.snohetta.com/)

- Strong: navigation recedes and the work is treated as the brand.
- Avoid copying: extreme minimalism and image dependency can hide the offer and fail when media is delayed.
- Template avoidance: no standard consultancy service grid dominates the opening.
- Typography: matter-of-fact labels allow the project imagery to lead.
- Proof: project archive is the homepage structure.
- Mobile: media must retain an intentional crop and immediate context.
- Motion: image sequencing supports discovery rather than explaining a generic process.

## White Arkitekter — one strong story at a time

Source: [White Arkitekter](https://whitearkitekter.com/se/)

- Strong: a single large story fills mobile and gives photography a clear editorial role.
- Avoid copying: KV lacks a comparable authentic image archive; generic substitutes would make this direction dishonest.
- Template avoidance: the first screen is not split into equal services or UI panels.
- Typography: large text sits directly on relevant media.
- Proof: reports and projects are named, not abstracted into claims.
- Mobile: the observed image crop, readable overlay and single CTA create a composed mobile screen.
- Motion: carousel movement must remain optional and controllable.

## Public Digital — confident public-sector voice

Source: [Public Digital](https://public.digital/)

- Strong: direct positioning and a simple organisation nameplate feel authoritative.
- Avoid copying: the observed mobile cookie layer covered much of the hero and a horizontal scrollbar was visible. KV must not reproduce either problem.
- Template avoidance: a bold colour field and institutional tone replace generic tech illustration.
- Typography: heavy sans-serif sets a clear public-sector voice.
- Proof: named client stories follow the positioning.
- Mobile: hierarchy is clear, but overlays and overflow damage the result.
- Motion: not required for the opening claim to work.

## Skylight — serious digital-government practice

Source: [Skylight](https://skylight.digital/)

- Strong: the offer is framed around better public services rather than technology trends.
- Avoid copying: US government language and credentials cannot be transferred to a Swedish consultancy.
- Template avoidance: plain explanations and real practice replace AI spectacle.
- Typography: accessible and pragmatic.
- Proof: experience and service outcomes are stated concretely.
- Mobile: clarity should survive without dense navigation.
- Motion: secondary to readable evidence.

## Framna — product proof and craft

Source: [Framna](https://framna.com/)

- Strong: digital product work is central and visually confident.
- Avoid copying: award rows and large-scale credentials would create false social proof for KV.
- Template avoidance: product experiences supply the visual language.
- Typography: contemporary sans-serif, controlled rather than slogan-heavy.
- Proof: real digital products and recognisable outcomes lead.
- Mobile: product imagery must stay legible rather than becoming tiny decorative phones.
- Motion: transitions should help inspect products, not delay them.

## Transferable direction for KV

1. Show Lägesbild and accurately labelled MÄSSY earlier.
2. Let one proof item dominate instead of rendering every dataset as equal modules.
3. Use white and living light blue as the requested brand field, with deep KV blue for structure.
4. Keep the interface quiet and avoid pseudo-technical decoration.
5. Build a strong no-motion first frame; add motion only around verified artefacts.
6. Compose mobile independently and test actual media crops.
