# Desktop composition

No composition is approved. The recommendation below describes Direction D if selected for Gate 2.

## Navigation

- White header with the supplied KV logo and the company name only.
- No “Utbilda · Införa · Bygga” text beside the logo.
- No numbered items or arrows on ordinary links.
- One plain contact link; avoid a generic rounded “book meeting” button.
- Navigation remains visually quiet so the work surface begins immediately.

## Hero

- Use a 12-column field without a conventional copy-left/card-right split.
- A faint oversized K or V derived from the real mark controls image cropping.
- Real Lägesbild screens overlap the letterform and remain legible.
- White remains dominant; luminous cyan is a moving light field, not a background gradient over the entire page.
- Natural explanatory copy replaces category eyebrow and slogan chains.
- Verified product status sits next to the product, not as a fake interface badge.

## Capability presentation

- Do not use three equal cards.
- Use an uneven continuum with one dominant current need and two supporting entry points.
- A visitor can enter through training, implementation or development without pretending every project follows all three.
- The final labels and details must fit real service content, not abstract outcomes.

## Proof

- Lägesbild is the first dominant project.
- Product screens are large enough to inspect.
- MÄSSY is visually secondary and explicitly labelled as a demonstration.
- New case material is added only with owner permission and factual challenge, intervention, deliverable and result.

## People

- Names and direct contact should not become three equal profile cards.
- If authentic portraits are supplied, use one composed team image or asymmetric portrait sequence.
- Until then, use a concise typographic contact treatment and do not invent roles.

## CTA

- One cyan field can conclude the page.
- Explain the first conversation in a complete sentence.
- Email and phone remain actionable.
- Avoid decorative arrows, pills and commitment-heavy language.

## Desktop rejection checklist

- Does a real product appear in the first viewport?
- Does any section default to three equal blocks?
- Is cyan used as meaningful brand energy rather than generic tech glow?
- Are there too many large headings competing for attention?
- Would the page still feel like KV if the logo were hidden?
